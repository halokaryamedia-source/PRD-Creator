AFTERSHOCK

Gameplay & Development Specification

Aftershock is a restoration adventure set in Emberhold, a steampunk city built across floating islands. A destructive storm has disabled the city’s machines, destroyed the original Beacon Core, and disconnected the power network. Each player follows one continuous journey from the lobby to Emberhold, restores the city through six connected gameplay stages, and returns after the assigned arena has been safely reset.

Session ModelUp to 5 concurrent players
5 independent arenas · 1 player each
Target PlaytimeApproximately 45 minutes
No global hard timeout
Game Structure6 connected gameplay stages
4 scored objectives

Complete Gameplay Journey

01The Docks

Arrive by airship, restore the dock route, and receive Brann’s mission.

02The Quarry

Mine Bronze, Silver, and Gold to manufacture a new Beacon Core.

03The Ascent

Carry the protected Core through four mountain hazard checkpoints.

04The Beacon

Produce repair materials and rebuild the Main Beacon through two storms.

05Power Relay

Reconnect five Power Nodes while repeated storms damage the network.

06Emberhold Restored

Witness the restored city, meet Brann, and return to the lobby.

Global Gameplay Direction
  • No combat, player death, precision parkour, or global failure timer.
  • Only one main objective is active at a time and every setback has a recovery path.
  • Important items cannot be permanently lost or duplicated.
  • Objective values are recorded for scoring and session data, while gameplay guidance remains focused on progress and decisions.

The Journey Begins

Emberhold, the approaching storm, and the route into the city

The adventure begins in the lobby and follows one uninterrupted restoration route through Emberhold. The story establishes the city’s damage first, then introduces each mechanic only when the player reaches the location where that mechanic is needed.

From the Lobby to Emberhold

The player begins in the lobby, where the Start Gate serves as the entrance to a new session. Using the Start Gate reserves one arena that is ready for play and begins the opening airship sequence. Up to five players may start at the same time, but each player travels through a separate copy of Emberhold, so progress, weather, items, scoring, and reset activity remain independent.

A steampunk airship carries the player away from the lobby and toward Emberhold. The city is built across floating islands connected by industrial routes, lifts, and power infrastructure. During the approach, the player can see that large parts of the city are dark, machinery has stopped, several travel routes are broken, and the Main Beacon stands inactive at the summit.

The Crisis Facing the City

An earlier storm destroyed the original Beacon Core, damaged the Main Beacon, and severed the network that distributes power through Emberhold. The storm front has not fully passed. It will reach the summit and power district during the later objectives, creating hazards that interrupt repair work without creating a global time limit or permanent failure. The player may take longer than the target duration and can always continue until the restoration is complete.

The complete route rises through the city in a readable order. The airship lands at The Docks on the outer edge of Emberhold. From there, the player enters The Quarry inside the main island, climbs through The Ascent toward the summit, repairs the Main Beacon, travels to the Power Relay district, and finally returns to the restored city center before leaving for the lobby.

Arrival at the First Playable Area

As the airship reaches The Docks, the opening view shifts from the distant city to a damaged industrial entrance. The player sees one clear route leading away from the landing point and toward a broken walkway. The first gameplay sequence begins there, using the dock damage to introduce the interaction language that will be reused throughout the rest of the game.

The airship lands, the dock route becomes active, and the player begins the restoration journey at The Docks.

02 — Gameplay FlowThe Docks

Arrival at The Docks

Restore the entrance route and receive Brann’s mission

The Docks introduces the world through a short, one-way repair sequence. Each completed action reveals the next destination, allowing the player to understand how Aftershock communicates objectives before the first scored objective begins.

Repairing the Broken Gangway

After leaving the airship, the player follows the only active route toward a dock walkway with one section missing. This damaged walkway is the Broken Gangway. When the player reaches it, one Repair Plate is supplied in the hotbar. The plate matches the visible gap and can be placed only in that position. An incorrect placement is removed and the same plate is returned, allowing the player to try again without losing the required item.

Once the Repair Plate completes the gangway, the previous guidance clears and the route continues toward a closed Crane Gate. A marked lever beside the gate becomes usable. Pulling the lever opens the gate once and exposes the next part of the damaged dock facility.

Restoring Power to the Dock

Beyond the Crane Gate, the player finds a Power Component, a machine part needed by the dock’s inactive Power Terminal. The component becomes available only after the gate opens. After pickup, it cannot be dropped, stored, duplicated, or permanently lost. The route from the component to the terminal is short and visually direct, so the player understands where the part belongs.

Installing the Power Component activates the Power Terminal. Machinery and lighting in the immediate dock area respond, confirming that the installation worked, and the final route toward the briefing area opens. The dock sequence has now taught movement, block placement, lever use, item pickup, protected-item handling, and item installation through one continuous repair problem.

Meeting Foreman Brann

At the end of the restored dock route, the player meets Foreman Brann, the engineer coordinating Emberhold’s recovery. Brann explains that repairing the dock is only the first step. The city cannot be restarted because the original Beacon Core was destroyed, and a replacement must be manufactured before the Main Beacon at the summit can be rebuilt.

Brann gives the player a Steampunk Communicator in a fixed hotbar slot. Using the Communicator opens a contextual menu that shows only actions relevant to the current situation. During the journey it carries Brann’s guidance, unlocks Request Hint and the Emergency Air Balloon when those forms of help become available, and provides Exit Session after the tutorial is complete.

The final dock gate opens after Brann’s briefing and Communicator handoff. The new route leads directly into The Quarry, where the materials for the replacement Beacon Core can be collected and processed.

The Docks is restored, Brann’s mission is established, and the player enters The Quarry with the Communicator.

02 — Gameplay FlowThe Quarry — Objective 1

Creating the Beacon Core

Mine three materials and assemble the replacement power core

The Quarry is the first scored objective and the first large production space. It turns Brann’s mission into a three-stage material process that ends with a physical Beacon Core the player must carry through the rest of the journey.

Entering the Vertical Mine

The route from The Docks opens into a central Forge Hub built inside Emberhold’s main island. Bronze Cave, Silver Cave, and Gold Cave occupy separate levels around the hub and connect through one automatic lift. Each cave contains a large natural chamber and smaller side tunnels, while the lift remains the main landmark used to return to the Forge.

When the player arrives, only Bronze Cave is available. The Bronze gate is open, the Silver and Gold gates remain closed, and the lift offers only the currently active destination. Entering the lift carries the player directly to Bronze Cave. The active-cave panel and objective display repeat the same material name so the player can confirm the current task before leaving the hub.

Mining Bronze for the Core Casing

The Forge supplies a protected Quarry Pickaxe in a fixed hotbar slot. The tool has no durability loss and can break only the ore assigned to the active cave. It cannot damage the surrounding environment or mine material from a locked or completed cave. Every valid ore block produces one matching ore item.

Bronze Cave contains 27 active Bronze Ore. Twenty-four ore are required to form the structural casing of the new Beacon Core, while three additional ore are optional surplus. Required progress and surplus progress are shown separately. Surplus is never needed to open the next cave, but every surplus ore submitted to the Forge provides additional score.

After collecting at least 24 Bronze Ore, the player returns to the Forge Hub and interacts with the Forge console. If Bronze Ore still remains in the cave, the Forge offers Exchange Now or Continue Mining. Continue Mining keeps Bronze Cave active. Exchange Now submits all Bronze Ore currently carried, uses 24 for the casing, records the remaining submitted amount as surplus, closes Bronze Cave, and unlocks Silver Cave.

Building the Conductive Assembly

The lift now offers Silver Cave as the only active destination. Silver Cave contains 33 active Silver Ore: 30 required and three optional surplus. The same mining and exchange process repeats, but the submitted Silver is used to create the conductive assembly inside the Beacon Core. When the Silver exchange is complete, Silver Cave closes and Gold Cave becomes available.

Completing the Power Channel

Gold Cave contains 42 active Gold Ore. Thirty-six are required to form the Core’s primary power channel, and six provide optional additional score. After the player chooses when to complete the final exchange, the Forge combines the Bronze casing, Silver conductive assembly, and Gold power channel into one replacement Beacon Core.

The finished Beacon Core appears in the visible Forge output. The objective does not end until the player personally collects it. Pickup removes the Quarry Pickaxe, secures the Beacon Core so it cannot be dropped or stored, and opens the lift route leading out of the mine. Brann confirms through the Communicator that the new Core must now be carried to the Main Beacon at the summit.

The replacement Beacon Core is complete, and the player leaves The Quarry through the lift exit toward The Ascent.

02 — Gameplay FlowThe Ascent — Objective 2

The Ascent to the Summit

Carry the protected Core through readable mountain hazards

The Ascent connects the underground Forge to the summit. This mountain gameplay focuses on reading warnings, learning safe routes, and recovering from mistakes without death, combat, precision jumping, or a complete-route restart.

Learning the Mountain’s Hazard Language

The player leaves the Quarry with the protected Beacon Core and reaches the first stable checkpoint platform. A checkpoint becomes active when the player reaches its safe platform, and that platform becomes the recovery reference for the next hazard segment. The first route contains Faithless Stone, a path block that shows cracks or particles before collapsing.

Checkpoint 1 deliberately demonstrates the danger. A long warning leads into one scripted safe fall. The player lands in a protected recovery area and follows a short return route that completes the tutorial segment and leads forward to Checkpoint 2. This Tutorial Fall teaches the recovery process but does not count as a real failure, does not reduce score, and does not unlock assistance.

Crossing the Unstable Route

Checkpoint 2 expands the Faithless Stone pattern across a wider path. When a block has already revealed whether it is stable or unsafe, that result remains consistent for the current objective attempt. After a fall, the player can use what was learned rather than facing an entirely new route.

Checkpoint 3 adds falling snow and ice above the collapsing path. An overhead cue appears before impact. A snow hit applies temporary Slowness, but the route remains wide enough to continue without precision movement. Reaching the safe platform at the end activates the next checkpoint and updates the recovery position.

Checkpoint 4 forms the final climb. Warnings become shorter, the Faithless Stone pattern becomes denser, and one directional avalanche pushes the player toward an approved landing area before the summit. A real fall or avalanche is counted once, preserves the Beacon Core, and returns the player to the active checkpoint after a short recovery instead of sending the player back to the beginning of The Ascent.

Receiving Help through the Communicator

After the first real fall on the current checkpoint, Request Hint appears in the Communicator. Using it briefly highlights the safe route for that checkpoint. Three hints are available across the entire Ascent, so unused help can be saved for a later segment.

After five total real falls or after the third hint is used, the Emergency Air Balloon becomes available. The player may ignore the offer and continue climbing. If the option is selected during an attempt, the sequence begins immediately, safely stages the player at the active checkpoint pickup position, and carries both the player and Beacon Core to the summit without requiring an additional walk to a separate station.

Reaching the Summit

The standard route ends at the Signal Horn. Reaching the horn is not enough by itself; the player manually interacts with it to confirm completion and signal Brann that the Beacon Core has arrived. The Balloon route reaches the same summit transition after its pickup cinematic. Both routes preserve the Core and open the path into The Beacon repair arena.

The player reaches the summit with the Beacon Core and enters the damaged Main Beacon complex.

02 — Gameplay FlowThe Beacon — Objective 3

Rebuilding the Main Beacon

Use the Core as temporary power while the storm reaches the summit

At the summit, the Beacon Core becomes a temporary power source for three repair machines. The player produces three kinds of repair material, rebuilds the central structure, and adapts when the approaching storm damages completed work.

Understanding the Summit Repair Arena

The Main Beacon stands at the center of the summit arena. Its missing sections are shown by a Ghost Scaffold, a projected repair guide that displays each empty position and the material required there. Three Repair Machines form a readable triangle around the Beacon, and a visible bunker provides shelter from the storm.

Each Repair Machine produces one functional material family: a structural material for the Beacon frame, a conductive material for internal power transfer, and a protective material for the outer system. The exact Minecraft blocks may change with the final Beacon design, but the visual identity of each material remains consistent between its machine, Output Tray, carried block, scaffold position, and installed state.

Producing and Installing Repair Materials

The permanent power lines to the machines are broken, so the player inserts the Beacon Core into one machine at a time. A powered machine changes from Inactive — No Core to Active — Producing. After a full eight-second cycle, one repair block appears in the front Output Tray. The tray stores up to eight blocks, and a full tray pauses the machine until space is cleared.

Removing the Beacon Core before a production cycle finishes cancels that partial cycle. The player moves the Core between machines according to the materials still needed, collects finished blocks from the trays, and installs them into matching Ghost Scaffold positions. A wrong material is removed and returned immediately, while correct placement remains visible as completed progress.

The Storm Reaches the Summit

The Beacon timer begins when all three Repair Machines are ready and the storm schedule starts. Brann warns through the Communicator that the same storm front threatening Emberhold is reaching the summit. The first visible warning begins at 2:50, impact occurs at 3:00, and the storm lasts until 3:15.

At impact, every machine changes to Paused — Storm and any unfinished production cycle returns to zero. Storm 1 destroys 25 percent of the scaffold positions that are filled at that moment, rounded upward. For example, two of eight filled positions are destroyed, while one filled position still results in one destroyed block. Damage is selected once at impact; blocks installed after that impact are not selected again by the same storm.

The player may keep moving the Core, collecting stored blocks, and rebuilding during the storm. Entering the bunker produces a clear Sheltered confirmation. While sheltered, the player avoids Slowness, Blindness, and the exposure penalty. The bunker does not prevent machine pause, production reset, or damage to blocks already installed on the Beacon. The Core, blocks carried in inventory, and blocks already stored in Output Trays remain safe.

Recovering and Completing the Beacon

After the first storm ends, every active machine begins its next output only after a new full production cycle. The player reproduces destroyed materials and continues repairing. Storm 2 follows the same sequence with a warning at 5:50, impact at 6:00, and an end at 6:15, but it destroys 35 percent of currently filled positions, rounded upward.

When every Ghost Scaffold position contains the correct material, completion immediately cancels any pending warning or active storm. The Beacon Core is removed from the machine or inventory and installed into the Main Beacon, unused repair materials are cleared, and the activation sequence begins. The restored Beacon can now generate power, but the city remains dark because the distribution network is still disconnected.

The Main Beacon activates and a controlled transition carries the player to the Power Relay district.

02 — Gameplay FlowPower Relay — Objective 4

Reconnecting Emberhold

Rebuild five power routes while repeated storms disrupt the network

Power Relay is the final scored objective. The Main Beacon now produces energy, but the city cannot use it until five separate connections are rebuilt and kept active at the same time.

Entering the Distribution District

The player arrives near the Main Power Source, the origin of Emberhold’s distribution network. Five Power Nodes are positioned around the district, with a Storage Room on one side, one shelter near the supplies, and a second shelter near the farthest node. The area is arranged so the player can understand the network from the source, then travel outward to each destination.

The original cable routes remain visible as damaged conduit frames and projected repair outlines called Ghost Cable Paths. Each path connects the Main Power Source to one Power Node and uses a distinct color: red, blue, green, yellow, or purple. The same color appears at the source, along the route, and on the destination node.

Collecting and Placing Cable

The Storage Room contains eight fixed chests supplied by an automatic maintenance system. Together the chests hold all five cable colors, with enough material for every path and a small spare allowance. The exact chest distribution changes between approved layouts, so the player must search the room rather than memorizing one container.

The player carries cable from the Storage Room and places each color on its matching Ghost Cable Path. An incorrect color or placement is removed and returned. When every position on one path is correct, its Power Node activates automatically and the active-node display increases. If any cable later disappears from that path, only the connected node becomes inactive.

The Repeating Storm Cycle

The first storm cycle begins when the Storage Room opens. The cycle runs for 75 seconds, with a visible warning during the final ten seconds. At impact, the game first checks whether the player is inside either shelter. A player outside shelter receives Slowness and Blindness, records one exposure for that storm, and loses all carried cable.

At the same impact, one currently active connection is chosen. Every player-built cable block on that path is removed, the related Power Node becomes inactive, and the affected color is identified so the player knows which route must be rebuilt. A shelter protects the player and carried inventory, but it does not protect the selected connection in the world. If no node is active when impact occurs, no path is destroyed, although the storm and resource effects still continue.

The storm remains active for 15 seconds. Cable cannot remain in the inventory while the player is outside shelter during that period, so cable collected while still exposed is removed on the next check. The player may continue opening chests and placing cable, but carrying new resources safely requires reaching a shelter.

Refreshing Supplies and Restoring the Network

When the storm ends, the maintenance system returns every cable color to its full chest supply and redistributes the items using another approved layout. Cable protected inside a shelter remains with the player even when the chests refill. The next 75-second cycle begins, and the player searches again, rebuilds the destroyed connection, and continues toward five active nodes.

The objective ends the moment all five Power Nodes are active together. Any warning, storm, cable damage, or pending chest refresh is canceled immediately. The Relay locks further placement, Brann confirms through the Communicator that power is reaching the city, and the final presentation begins.

All five Power Nodes are active, allowing the Main Beacon to distribute energy throughout Emberhold.

02 — Gameplay FlowEmberhold Restored — Ending

Emberhold Restored

Show the result of the journey and return safely to the lobby

The ending closes the restoration story before technical cleanup begins. The player first sees the city recover, then meets Brann, receives the completion presentation, and leaves Emberhold.

Returning to the Restored City

When the fifth Power Node activates, all Power Relay activity stops and a controlled transition moves the player to an approved viewpoint in the city center. The route avoids other arenas, unloaded spaces, and technical reset areas so the final scene remains focused on Emberhold.

The restoration appears in a deliberate order. Nearby city lights and local machines activate first, showing that power has reached the surrounding district. The Main Beacon and Power Relay then illuminate as connected systems, followed by environmental effects that reveal Emberhold operating as a complete city again.

Closing Brann’s Story

After the restoration is clearly visible, Foreman Brann appears physically at the city center. He acknowledges that the player created the replacement Beacon Core, carried it to the summit, rebuilt the Main Beacon, and restored all five distribution routes. The Steampunk Hero Badge is then presented once as a celebration of the completed journey. It is not a rank, permanent inventory item, or persistent unlock.

Leaving Emberhold

The complete session result is stored while the ending presentation remains active. After storage is safe, temporary gameplay items, effects, and interface elements are removed behind the transition. The player returns to the lobby without seeing technical cleanup or an unfinished arena state.

Once the player has left, the assigned arena restores every gameplay area to its original condition. Gates, lift routes, ore, hazards, machines, scaffold positions, cable paths, chests, nodes, dialogue, weather, timers, and temporary items are verified before the arena becomes available again. The next journey begins only when a player uses the Start Gate after that verification is complete.

Emberhold is restored, the completed session is safely closed, and the arena is prepared for a new independent journey.

Development Overview

AFTERSHOCK is one connected gameplay journey from The Docks to Emberhold Restored. The map supports Multi Session through the Game System: several players can play at the same time, while every player owns a separate Session, Assigned Arena, progress, items, timers, scores, entities, and world changes. Development must connect every gameplay package, preserve valid data between objectives, handle Exit or disconnect safely, store the final result, and return the used arena to its starting condition.

Development Flow

01Game System Setup

Create the Multi Session system, assign one separate arena to each active player, and keep every session isolated.

02Gameplay Package Setup

Connect The Docks, Objective 1–4, and Ending in the required order with clear entry and completion conditions.

03Data and Scoring Setup

Store Session Data, important items, objective progress, Objective Scores, and Final Total under the correct player session.

04Reset and Reuse

After a session finishes or stops, clear temporary state, reset only its Assigned Arena, verify the result, and release the arena.
